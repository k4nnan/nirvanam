Every piece plants trees

Nirvanam Studio is the shop for a longer project: bringing two pieces of land back to life, a small woodland in West Wales and a coconut grove in South India. 10% of the profit on every piece we sell pays for planting and caring for trees on that land.

The furniture is part of the same idea. Every piece already exists. Giving it a second life means no new tree is felled to make it.

West Wales

A woodland to open up

Dense, evenly planted rows with older broadleaf woodland on the edges. Planted that close, little light reaches the ground and little else can grow. Over the next ten years we are giving it room.

  • Native oak, birch, rowan, hazel and hawthorn planted in clearings and along the rides
  • A new hedgerow along the field edge
  • Ponds dug for wildlife
  • Timber we thin made into furniture in our own workshop on the land

South India

From coconut rows to food forest

A coconut grove beside an open stretch of bare, grazed ground. Palms on their own give thin shade and tired soil. We are layering it into a food forest where many kinds of tree grow together.

  • Native neem, tamarind and pongamia replanting the open ground
  • Jackfruit, mango and shade trees between the palms
  • Mulch and ground cover to rebuild the soil and hold the monsoon rain
  • Rainwater kept on the land, not run off it

Honest about carbon

Moving furniture across an ocean has a carbon cost, and we do not claim to be carbon neutral yet. This is what we do today, and what we are working towards.

What we do now

  • Reuse before anything newEvery piece already exists. Keeping it in use saves the timber, the making and the waste.
  • Sea, never airPieces travel by ship, a small fraction of the emissions of air freight for the same weight.
  • Full containersWe ship together, so each piece carries a smaller share of the journey.

What we are working towards

  • Measure every shipmentThe emissions of each journey, from Karaikudi to your door.
  • Plant more than we emitEnough trees on our own land to cover those emissions, then more.
  • Publish the numbersHere, once a year, including where we fall short.
